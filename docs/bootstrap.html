RUM Bootstrap Inference

Can the observed choice probabilities have come from a random utility model? The bootstrap answers with a p-value for H0: dist = 0, using Hong and Li's numerical delta method, and with two confidence intervals for the distance: their one-sided interval and the two-sided interval of the paper's application section. Every number is certified: it comes from the brackets lb ≤ dist ≤ ub that the RUM solver proves. This page shows the two input styles, the pieces and how they call the solver, the defaults, the design choices, and how each part was checked (§10); the results are in §8.

1The test in one picture

Here dist(p) is the distance from p to the RUM polytope on the observed rows, the quantity calculate_distance brackets. The bootstrap needs it at the observed p̂ and at B perturbed points Pb. The formulas are Hong and Li's numerical delta method, as in the reference implementation the bootstrap was checked against:

r_n = √n ε_n = n^(−1/3) s = 1 + ε_n·r_n DSR n = 520: s = 3.836 · AI n = 100: s = 3.154 P_b = p̂ + ε_n·r_n·(p̂_b − p̂) p̂_b: the b-th bootstrap draw of p̂, b = 1 … B T = r_n · dist(p̂) p = #{ b : dist(P_b) ≥ τ } / B, τ = s · dist(p̂) reject H₀ at α when p ≤ α CI = [ dist(p̂) − q_(1−α) / r_n , ∞ ) q_(1−α): the (1−α)-quantile of (dist(P_b) − dist(p̂)) / ε_n

The solver never returns dist exactly. It returns a certified bracket: dist(p̂) ∈ [L0, U0] and dist(Pb) ∈ [Lb, Ub]. So the threshold τ becomes a band, and each draw falls into one of three cases:

p ∈ [ #above / B , (#above + #undecided) / B ] reported p = the upper end c = 1 / (ε_n·r_n) so CI lower end = (1 + c)·dist(p̂) − c·Q_(1−α)(dist(P_b)) low ∈ [ (1 + c)·L₀ − c·Q(U) , (1 + c)·U₀ − c·Q(L) ] reported = the left end; Q(·) = np.quantile(·, 1 − α)

Both reported values equal the reference's when the brackets collapse (the exact QPs). The difference from the reference is what happens to undecided draws and to the draws that decide the quantile: here they are re-solved at a tighter tolerance until the bracket is narrow (§3, step 6), instead of being left wide.

2Components

Three modules in solver/inference/ and one class, Session, in solver/rum_solver.py. The input is one table of choices, a panel or replies. cells_from_panel counts the menus, alternatives and p̂ in it, the RUM object is built from those, and rum.bootstrap() gets the same table for its draws, after checking that the object's p̂ equals the table's counts (to 1e-9). The math has no solver in it, the draws have no math in them, and only Session touches a solver.

# solver/inference/draws.py: menus, alternatives and p̂, counted from the panel
menu, alt, p_hat = cells_from_panel(panel, 25, alternatives=[0])
# solver/rum_solver.py: the solver class, as for calculate_distance
rum = RUM(menu, alt, p_hat, num_alternatives=25)
# solver/inference/bootstrap.py: a Bootstrap with its draws; nothing is solved yet
boot = rum.bootstrap(panel=panel)
# the certificate, the screen and the solves; returns a PValue
p = boot.p_value()

3One run, step by step

What boot.p_value() does the first time it is called. A later confidence_interval() reuses steps 1–5 and every bracket already found.

  1. Plan once.

    rum.session(method, method_args) resolves the method (automatic choice: AI → bm_admm at 3.1 % density, DSR → flow_cg), its storage format and its GPU budget, exactly as calculate_distance would. Every solve below uses this one plan, so the certificate of p̂ and all B draws share the method and dtype.

  2. Certify p̂ and keep its state.

    s.solve(p̂, abs_tol, rel_tol, keep=True) returns [L0, U0], the fitted point x̂ (a RUM point, so ‖x̂ − p‖ bounds dist(p) from above for every p) and the warm state: flow_cg's columns and weights, bm_admm's ADMM state. The τ band follows. If U0 ≤ 10−9, the data are rationalizable, p = 1 and no draw is solved (the reference's short cut).

  3. Draw.

    The respondent weights W (B × R) are drawn once, from numpy.random.default_rng(seed). Pb is built in blocks of at most 128 MB: all 10,000 draws at once for DSR, 228 at a time for AI, whose whole B × M matrix would be 5.9 GB. W is 8 MB for AI and 42 MB for DSR.

  4. Screen every draw at once.

    Ub = ‖x̂ − Pb‖ for all b, vectorized. Where Ub < τlo the draw is certified below with no solve. Measured on DSR: 2,378 of 10,000 draws (alt0) and 1,055 (full). On AI the screen decides every draw (the largest Ub is 0.555 τlo on gpt-5.6-luna and 0.682 τlo on deepseek-v4-flash).

  5. Solve the rest, one draw at a time.

    s.solve(P_b, below=τ_lo, above=τ_hi, warm=True) starts from p̂'s state and stops at the first of: Ub < τlo, Lb ≥ τhi, the tolerance (undecided), or draw_time. The engine's stop only ends the solve early. The decision itself is always made from the certified bracket, in hong_li.decide.

  6. Refine what is still open.

    The tolerance drops tenfold, up to refine times. In each round, p̂ is re-certified first when undecided draws sit inside the τ band, which no draw tolerance can resolve. Then only the undecided draws are re-solved. On DSR full, 13 draws are undecided at 5e-4; the rounds re-solve only those, then the few still open, and settle all of them.

  7. Aggregate.

    The p-value bracket, the decision at α and the counts. For the CI, the certified quantile of §6. The run record carries the settings, the plan line, the fingerprints of data and draws, every draw's bracket and decision, and the times of each step.

The two inputs

Why DSR needs the respondent and AI does not

A draw must resample whatever was sampled independently. The two data sets differ in what that is.

  • AI: one call. Each menu was asked a fixed number of times by design: 100 (gpt-5.6-luna, deepseek-v4-flash) or 2,000 (gpt-6 luna, GMI Cloud). Every ask is a fresh API call with no memory of the others, and the display order is reshuffled each time, so a menu's replies are independent draws from one choice distribution, independent across menus. Nothing links one reply to another, so each menu's replies are resampled on their own.
  • What the AI table needs. menu and chosen, one row per reply. A menu's number of replies ND is its number of rows, and the draws use only how often each alternative was chosen on each menu: on {6, 9}, gpt-6 luna chose watch 9 in 1,953 of the 2,000 asks. The replicate column is the ask's number, not a count, and is ignored. What makes this valid is the independence, not the fixed count: a menu that lost unparseable replies uses the ones it kept (GMI Cloud: 1,995 to 2,000 per menu).
  • DSR: one person. 520 respondents each answered 10 menus, the full 25-alternative menu and 9 of the other 300: 5,200 rows, and 6 to 520 answers per menu (median 15). The randomness is which people were sampled, and a person's ten answers come from the same tastes, so they are linked. A draw picks 520 people with replacement, each with all ten answers, which keeps the link. Treating the 5,200 answers as independent would understate how much p̂ varies, narrow the bootstrap distribution and over-reject.
  • What the DSR table needs. respondent, menu and chosen, one row per answer; how many answers a person gave follows from the rows. Because a draw picks people, a menu's number of answers changes from draw to draw and can reach 0; multinomial_reject redraws such a resample (72 times at B = 10,000).

The AI replies were collected as jsonl, one line per call. replication/empirical_ai_n18/replies.py turns them into a table: respondent = replicate, menu = the set shown, chosen = the product at the reply's position, unparseable replies dropped (none for gpt-5.6-luna, 165 for deepseek-v4-flash). final_data/Application_AI/raw_data/ holds these tables as .npz. The same table serves as the panel, or, its respondent column ignored, as the replies.

The replies

  • Form. Two columns, menu and chosen, one row per reply, with the same rules as the panel's; any other column, such as a replicate number, is ignored.
  • The draws. Each draw redraws every menu's ND replies with replacement, on its own: menu D's counts are Multinomial(ND, D's shares), independent across menus. Block k of the draws comes from default_rng([seed, k]), so any block is rebuilt alone. A full pass over the 10,000 AI draws takes 28 s (Luna) and 39 s (DeepSeek) on one core, against about 9 s for the panel.
  • The same seed gives the same draws only with the same blocks. A block holds at most 128 MB of draws (BLOCK_BYTES in draws.py): 228 draws for the AI data's 73,492 cells, so draws 0 to 227 come from block 0's random stream, draws 228 to 455 from block 1's, and so on, 44 blocks for B = 10,000. With another BLOCK_BYTES, or a RUM object with another number of cells, the blocks are cut elsewhere and the same seed gives other draws: just as valid, but not the record's, so the interval can move in its last digits. The record keeps a fingerprint of all B draws (16 hex digits of their sha256), which shows whether a re-run used the same draws, and a checkpoint will not resume with different ones. The panel draws do not depend on the block size: the respondent weights are drawn all at once, and the blocks only cut them up.
  • n. The number of replies per menu: 100 or 2,000 for AI. Unless given it is the most replies on any menu (deepseek-v4-flash: 100, though a few menus kept 94 to 99 after dropped replies; GMI Cloud: 2,000, with 1,995 to 2,000 kept); a given n must lie between the fewest and the most. weights does not apply.
  • When not to use it. Never for DSR-style data: a person's answers are linked, and resampling each menu on its own would ignore that.

5The solver contract

The bootstrap asks one thing of the solver: bracket dist at this point, and stop as soon as the bracket clears a threshold. Session.solve answers through the same planner and runners as calculate_distance. Each runner gets three optional extras: threshold stops, a warm start, and a request to keep state. When none are given, the runner runs exactly as in calculate_distance.

The parts that must match, and how they do

Method and dtype
Planned once per session and recorded in every result. The p̂ certificate and all draws share them. Re-planning per draw could switch formats if GPU memory changed in between; the session never re-plans.
Bound precision
ub is the float64 distance of a RUM point; lb is float64 wherever a float64 form fits. A decision that rests on a lower bound with lb_rigorous False (flow_cg at n = 34–35 on 32 GB) is counted and named in the result.
Tolerances
The class's rule: stop when abs_gap ≤ abs_tol or rel_gap ≤ rel_tol. The applications used abs 5e-4 and rel 0, which is the default here.
The distance
One definition: ‖·‖ on the observed rows, singleton rows as a constant, vcov passed through (Gurobi methods only).
Row order
Input row order everywhere. The session maps to and from each engine's order, so x̂, the draws and Pb line up with rum.menu.
The [0, 1] check
calculate_distance keeps rejecting p outside [0, 1], because it takes observed probabilities. Session.solve accepts any finite vector. It has to: on DSR every one of the 10,000 perturbed points has entries outside [0, 1] (measured on alt0 and full).
Decisions
Made in one place, hong_li.decide(L_b, U_b, τ_lo, τ_hi), with the reference's strict rules (below iff Ub < τlo; above iff Lb ≥ τhi). Engine stop reasons are only used to stop early, never to decide. For example, bm_admm's target test is ≤, not <.
Seeds and time
The draws' seed is separate from flow_cg's first-column seed. Time is reported per step: certificate, screen, loop, refinement. Setup is counted once.

6Making it fast

A run costs one certificate of p̂, a screen of every draw, and the solves of the draws the screen leaves open. How many it leaves open depends on the data. On the AI data the screen decides all 10,000 draws, so the p-value costs one certificate and a pass over the draws (21.5 s to 130.9 s on the four data sets, §8). On DSR the draws scatter further from the p̂ fit, and 7,622 (alt0) and 8,945 (full) of them need a solve.

The levers, in order of effect

  • Restart from p̂. Every draw starts from p̂'s solver state (on the AI data at B = 1,000, BM-ADMM took 300 s from a cold start and 10 s restarted).
  • Screen all draws with the p̂ fit. One vectorized pass gives every draw a certified upper bound. This is the restart test run for every draw at once, with no solver call.
  • Stop at the decision. A draw stops when its bracket clears the band; below-decisions never pay for a lower bound.
  • Refine only what is open. Undecided draws and the p̂ certificate are tightened; settled draws are never re-solved.
  • Plan and build once. One plan, one Gurobi model, a CuPy pool kept warm: setup is paid once per session instead of once per draw.
  • Stream the draws. W is kept; Pb is built in blocks of at most 128 MB. Memory stays at W plus one block.

The confidence interval needs a different kind of work

The p-value only needs each draw's side of τ. The CI needs the (1 − α)-quantile of dist(Pb), and only the draws near that quantile matter. The quantile is monotone in every draw, so Q(L) ≤ Q ≤ Q(U). A draw whose whole bracket lies outside the band [Q(L), Q(U)] cannot move either end. The CI therefore solves draws in descending order of their screen bound until the band has a lower end, then re-solves only the draws that overlap it, until the interval's bracket is about 2 × abs_tol wide.

9Design choices

Where the reference procedure leaves room, these are the choices made, and why.

1Where the draws come from

n and p̂ fix the scaling, but not the bootstrap distribution: resampling needs the choices behind p̂, who saw which menu and chose what. The reference's bootstrap_p_from_panel takes a units × cells 0/1 matrix and averages over all units, which assumes every unit answered every menu; a DSR respondent answered 10 of the 301.

Choice: the input is the long table of choices, in one of two styles (above): a panel, whose respondents are resampled, or replies, whose menus are resampled one by one. A cell's draw divides by the weighted number of times its menu was seen. Per-menu counts alone and precomputed draws are not accepted.

2The AI data: replicates or replies

Every ask of a menu, 100 or 2,000 of them, is a separate call, so there is no real respondent. Resampling replicate numbers is valid, since the replicates are independent copies of the design, but it ties every menu's draw to the same picks; resampling each menu on its own matches how the data were made.

Choice: both are available. The paper's AI p-values and the application tables use the replicate as the respondent; --input replies runs the menu-by-menu version, which the paper's AI intervals use. Both give p = 0 (§8).

3Form of the confidence interval

The reference gives the one-sided [lower, ∞), the dual of the test. Its lower end can be negative.

Choice: both. confidence_interval() is the reference's one-sided interval, with the lower end reported as computed and as max(0, ·). distance_interval() is the two-sided interval of the paper's application section, [max(0, L₀ − c), U₀ + c] with c the (1 − α)-quantile of ‖p̂b − p̂‖; the distance to a convex set moves by at most ‖p − p̂‖, so it needs no solve.

4Tolerance and refinement

The applications certify at abs 5e-4, which leaves a few DSR draws undecided: 2 on alt0, 13 on full.

Choice: abs 5e-4, rel 0, and up to two tenfold tighter rounds (5e-5, 5e-6) for p̂ and the draws still undecided. refine=0 keeps the reference protocol exactly.

5Cost of the confidence interval

The interval needs certified lower bounds near the (1 − α)-quantile. On AI the p-value costs one certificate and the screen, while the interval must certify several hundred upper-tail draws.

Choice: computed only when asked, to about 2 × abs_tol. DSR: 592 and 649 draws, 4 and 11 min. AI: about 600 draws on the 100-replicate data sets and 1,500 to 3,900 on the 2,000-replicate ones (estimates); it is not computed there, because the paper reports the two-sided interval, which costs no solve (§8).

6Stopping once the decision is locked

The reference's test mode stops as soon as the decision at α cannot change. On DSR (p ≈ 0.11) that would save about half the solves, but p would then be known only to a bracket.

Choice: p_value(stop_when_decided=True), off by default.

7The sample size n

n sets rn and εn. A panel knows its respondents (520 for DSR, 100 or 2,000 for AI as a panel), and replies know their replies per menu (100 or 2,000 for AI).

Choice: taken from the data unless given: the number of unique respondents, or the most replies on any menu. A given n is checked: it must equal the number of respondents, or lie between the fewest and the most replies on a menu.

8The application tables

final_results/Application_DSR and final_results/Application_AI list one row per run, solver by solver.

Choice: the GPU rows come from the rum.bootstrap records in replication/empirical_*/results/, with the intervals in the ci_* and dci_* columns; the IP-CG and IP-H-CG rows come from the cluster runs in replication/cgip/cluster_records/.

Cross-checks behind the results

  • DSR decisions. All 10,000 decisions of both specifications agree, draw by draw, with a run that solved every draw to abs 1e-5 and with the IP-CG and IP-H-CG cluster runs.
